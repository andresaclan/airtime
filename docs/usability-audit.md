# Airtime usability audit

I followed a first visit through marking two jumps, correcting frames, reviewing measurements, saving, reloading, and comparing sessions. I also tried a wrong original, a damaged video, an invalid backup, and an unsaved reload. The findings below describe the current interface in Chromium. The phone observations use touch emulation.

## Reading the workspace

- **The instructional cue can lag one step behind my task.** Immediately after I placed the plant, the panel said “Next: takeoff” while the hint still described the plant foot’s first contact. The distinction between the selected mark and the upcoming mark deserves a visible label.
- **The desktop layout separates the instruction from the action.** At 1280 × 800 the mark button sat under the video, the next step near the top of the sidebar, and its instruction at the bottom. Meanwhile the empty flight value occupied the most prominent type. Putting the instruction next to Mark would shorten the repeated glance between the athlete’s feet and the controls.
- **Portrait and landscape expose different information.** A widescreen clip in the tall phone layout had large black bands. Rotating to 844 × 390 freed much of the right rail for a large flight value, but removed the persistent frame number, FPS and audio controls. At 320 pixels wide the compact summary also lost the visible jump number on a selected mark. Keep frame and jump identity visible while correcting frames.
- **The marking vocabulary changes as I work.** The guide names Plant step, Takeoff and Landing. The ambiguous Mark menu offers only “Touchdown or landing” and “Takeoff”. A user who came back to add the missing plant has to infer which choice creates it. Name the resulting step where possible.
- **Zoom is easy to miss when it matters most.** Double-clicking the picture produced a useful 200% view and Reset control. Neither the 1× viewer nor the shortcuts dialog surfaced that gesture. A small zoom affordance would let a first-time user inspect shoe contact before making a mark.
- **Empty and incomplete results use competing counts.** With one initial mark, Results showed “0 jumps” above a row named “Jump 1”. With an empty workspace, the large flight value read “–ms”. Count completed and in-progress jumps explicitly and use the empty space for the next action.
- **The measurements need a short explanation.** My repeat-hop result showed 600 ms flight, 467 ms contact and RSI 0.95. The app explained frame precision but never expanded RSI in this flow. The onboarding also implies all three marks are necessary, although a takeoff and landing suffice for flight time. Explain what each mark unlocks and define RSI near its value.
- **A tie looks like a win.** For two jumps displayed as 4 ms, only Jump 1 received Best. If precision beyond the displayed value decides the winner, show it on inspection. If values are equal, identify the tie.
- **The success notice can contradict the current state.** Saving, immediately returning to Video and adding a mark produced “Saved” and “Unsaved” simultaneously. Clear the previous success notice as soon as the session becomes dirty.

## History, trends, and storage

- **Chart selection yields two answers on one screen.** In a two-session repeat-hop series, I selected January with the chart’s Home key. The chart headline still showed 267 ms and a decrease since January, while the selected January detail showed 600 ms. Label the headline as latest, or make the headline follow the selected session.
- **Every jump invites an interaction the chart does not provide.** The mode displayed the additional points, but the keyboard slider selected sessions, the details showed the session best, and the accessible table still described “best jump per session”. Offer individual-jump inspection or label the other points as visual context.
- **One-session charts look more conclusive than the data.** For a single short test flight the axes extended to negative flight and contact times, and showed dates on both sides of the only measurement. A single dated result would communicate the available information more clearly.
- **The home trend prompt can be misleading.** My dashboard said “Save more sessions to see a trend” for Drop jump while I already had two comparable Repeat hops sessions. Qualify the message by jump type and make existing trends discoverable.
- **Dates change meaning between screens.** I set Recorded to January 2, 2025 and saved in November 2026. The same session showed Jan 2 on Home, Nov 18 in the saved-session list and Jan 2, 2025 in Trends. The list showed no “last edited” label, and the short dates omitted the year. The default Recorded value also comes from the file’s modification timestamp. Label date provenance and include years across training seasons.
- **The save model takes effort to learn.** Reloading an unsaved edit brought back a dashboard with a draft, but the draft’s direct action was Discard. Recovery required choosing the original again through the general video picker. Deleting the currently open saved session also left the workspace open and marked Unsaved. Give drafts a Resume action and explain what deleting an open session leaves behind.
- **Backup controls compete in the empty state.** An empty Saved sessions dialog showed Import backup twice, disabled Export all, and instructed desktop users to press both platform variants of Save. One import action and the current platform’s shortcut would be enough.
- **Invalid inputs receive inconsistent feedback.** Importing a non-backup JSON produced “Not an Airtime export file”. Dropping a text file on the player did nothing visible. Apply the same direct feedback to video drops.
- **Navigation icons require trial and error.** In the desktop header, Sessions and Replace video were icon-only. On the phone, the saved-state word collapsed to a dot. The returning dashboard says Manage, the header says Sessions, and both lead to the same dialog. Consistent names and a visible state change would reduce guessing.

## Keyboard and playback details

- Opening Saved sessions during playback let the clip keep advancing behind the modal. Closing it returned me to a later frame. Pausing when a blocking dialog opens would preserve the moment I was inspecting.
- The List and Trends controls expose tab roles. Right Arrow from List left it selected. Support arrow-key navigation between tabs. Escape and focus restoration worked in the shortcuts dialog.
- After dismissing marking hints, the control to turn them back on appeared inside Keyboard shortcuts. Touch users looking for coaching have little reason to open a keyboard reference.

## Coverage and limits

Exercised first-run onboarding and its diagram, the returning dashboard and expandable guide, file selection and drag-and-drop, widescreen and portrait footage, constant and variable frame rate, a 240 fps MOV, play/pause, stepping, speed, mute, timeline navigation, touch scrubbing and slide-up marking, zoom/reset, all three jump types, mark selection and editing, undo/redo, results and metadata, help and overflow menus, drafts, saved-session matching, trend modes and date filters, keyboard chart selection, storage protection, export, duplicate import, invalid import, deletion and restoration from backup. The saved repeat-hop results were 467 and 600 ms, with a 533 ms average. A duplicate import correctly skipped the existing session and an import after deletion restored it.

Desktop testing used local Chromium at 1280 × 800. Compact layouts included 390 × 844, 320 × 568 and 844 × 390. The shared preview timed out. Generated clips and repository media fixtures exercised interaction and timing displays. Athletic-event identification, long-session performance, native Safari, physical-device gesture ergonomics, screen-reader speech output and browser-specific permission dialogs remain unverified.
